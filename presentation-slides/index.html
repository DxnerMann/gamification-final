StudyGarden

Grow your knowledge — one seed at a time.

A calm, garden-inspired study companion that keeps students in flow with micro-tasks, visible growth, and gentle community.

Start presenting See the concept
Keep learning and grow.
1 Problem

Students leave the flow channel

Studying either overwhelms (“prepare for the statistics exam”) or bores (monotonous repetition). Students procrastinate and drop out of the flow channel.

When challenge outpaces skill, anxiety rises. When skill outpaces challenge, boredom sets in. StudyGarden aims for the diagonal path between them.

Overwhelm → delay Monotony → quit Flow → continue
2 Goal

Keep students in flow

Break overwhelming tasks into small, doable steps and keep studying varied and fun — so learners start earlier and study continuously.

  • Split big exams into seeds students can finish today.
  • Keep variety high so boredom never takes root.
  • Encourage early starts and continuous care — not last-minute cramming.
3 Concept

Seeds → Care → Grow

Every module is a garden bed, every topic is a plant, every study session is care. Big tasks become “seeds” — 10–15 minute micro-tasks. Plants grow visibly as you learn.

4–5 Personas

Designed for two familiar learners

Different reasons to stall — one garden that meets both.

P
Player type: Achiever

The Procrastinator

Overwhelmed by the amount of material — doesn’t start at all. Seeds shrink the first step until starting feels safe.

B
Player type: Explorer / Socializer

The Bored One

Starts motivated, quits after a few days of repetitive tasks. Needs variety, rare plants, and the community garden.

6 Motivators

Intrinsic roots, extrinsic blooms

Balance deep reasons to learn with light collectible delight.

Intrinsic

  • Mastery — watching competence take shape
  • Curiosity — new plants and topics to discover
  • Autonomy — deciding which plant to tend
  • Purpose — helping others grow

Extrinsic

  • Rare plants worth hunting for
  • Collection progress you can see
  • Community recognition without toxic ranking
7 Fun

Delight that feels earned

The joy isn’t points — it’s a living garden of progress.

Watch it grow

Discover rares

Unexpected plants reward curiosity and variety.

Grow together

Friends and study groups share a community plot.

8 Player Journey

From balcony box to botanical garden

Three stages of identity as habits take root.

Stage 1

Seed

Onboarding · balcony box

  • Scale the first module
  • Split tasks into seeds
  • Celebrate the first sprout
Stage 2

Gardener

Habit building · own garden

  • Regular care sessions
  • Unlock new plants & tools
  • Take on harder plants
Stage 3

Botanist

Expert · botanical garden

  • Gift cuttings to beginners
  • Mentor through community
  • Study-group garden plots
9 Engagement Loop

A gentle cycle that pulls you back

Motivation → action → feedback → growth — then again.

10 Mechanics → Dynamics

What players do becomes how they feel

Each mechanic is chosen for a psychological outcome.

Seeds / micro-tasks→Small wins, no overwhelm
Growth stages→Visible progress
Plant variety / collection→Curiosity & variety
Community garden→Social commitment
Gifting cuttings→Purpose & mastery
11 Technologies

Quiet infrastructure for flow

Helpful systems stay in the background so the garden stays front and center.

  • AI seed assistant that splits big tasks into micro-tasks.
  • Adaptive difficulty tuned to the flow channel.
  • University LMS integration (Moodle / ILIAS) and calendar sync.
12 Ethics

Care without punishment

The garden never shames a student for resting.

  • Plants never die — they rest. No guilt for illness, exams, or breaks.
  • No study-time leaderboards — avoid competitive stress.
  • AI safeguard: explains and structures, never writes assignments.
  • Privacy: learning data is never shared with the university or lecturers.
